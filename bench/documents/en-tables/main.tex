\begin{abstract}
This document collects the kinds of tables that appear in technical papers:
plain grids, rule-only tables, merged cells, aligned numbers, formulas in
cells, and a table that breaks across pages. It exists to test how well a
converter keeps their structure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Merged cells}

Table~\ref{tab:merged} spans columns with \verb|\multicolumn| and rows with
\verb|\multirow|.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Accuracy by document type and layout.}
  \label{tab:merged}
  \begin{tabular}{llcc}
    \toprule
    & & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Accuracy (\%)} \\
    \cmidrule(lr){3-4}
    Type & Layout & Text & Structure \\
    \midrule
    \multirow{2}{*}{Paper}  & one column & 98.1 & 91.4 \\
                            & two columns & 93.7 & 84.2 \\
    \midrule
    \multirow{2}{*}{Report} & one column & 97.5 & 89.9 \\
                            & two columns & 92.0 & 80.6 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Formulas in cells}

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Common distances between strings $a$ and $b$.}
  \label{tab:math}
  \begin{tabular}{>{\raggedright}p{3.5cm}l}
    \toprule
    Name & Definition \\
    \midrule
    Normalized edit distance & $\dfrac{\operatorname{lev}(a,b)}{\max(|a|,|b|)}$ \\[1.2ex]
    Jaccard distance on tokens & $1 - \dfrac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|}$ \\[1.2ex]
    Character error rate & $\dfrac{S + D + I}{N}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Here $S$, $D$ and $I$ count substitutions, deletions and insertions, and $N$
is the length of the reference.
